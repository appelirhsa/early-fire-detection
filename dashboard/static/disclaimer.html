ACADEMIC PROJECT — NOT FOR PUBLIC OR IMMEDIATE USE This system is a student prototype. It is not a certified fire detection, alarm or suppression system and must not be relied on to protect people, property or equipment.
01 / PROJECT STATUS

An academic project

The Early Fire Detection and Monitoring System was designed and built by Aphiwe Elihle Mthembu (student number 223087197) as a Final Year Project in the Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering Science at the University of Johannesburg (Project Investigation (Electrical) 4B). It exists to demonstrate and evaluate engineering concepts for assessment and research.

The University of Johannesburg is named only as the institution at which the work was carried out. The system is not a product of the university, and the university has not endorsed, certified or approved it for any use.

02 / INTENDED USE

Not intended for public or immediate use

  • The system has not been certified, approved or independently tested as a fire detection, alarm or suppression system, and has not been assessed against SANS 10139 or any other fire-safety standard.
  • It must not be used as the sole, primary or replacement means of detecting fire or protecting life, property, data or equipment.
  • It must not replace or interfere with any certified fire detection, alarm or suppression system already installed in a building or server room.
  • It is intended only for supervised demonstration, testing and evaluation by the author and assessors.
03 / SUPPRESSION

Experimental suppression output

The prototype can switch a relay intended to operate a small water-mist valve or a stand-in device. Water near energised electrical equipment can cause electric shock, short circuits and damage. The suppression output is a proof of concept only and must not be connected to a building's suppression system, a mains water supply or live equipment outside a supervised test.

04 / ACCURACY AND AVAILABILITY

Readings and alerts may be wrong, late or missing

  • Temperatures come from a low-resolution 32 × 24 thermal sensor and an ambient sensor, and may be inaccurate, especially for small or distant heat sources.
  • The fire classification combines fixed temperature thresholds with an experimental machine-learning model trained on limited data. It can miss fires and raise false alarms.
  • This dashboard depends on Wi-Fi, the internet and a cloud server. Alerts shown here may be delayed or may not arrive at all, and the dashboard can only display what the sensing unit reports.
05 / DATA

Data collected

The system stores low-resolution thermal readings, ambient temperatures and event records on the project server for monitoring, testing and model training. At 32 × 24 pixels, people appear only as warm shapes. Data is used solely for this academic project and is not sold or shared with third parties.

07 / EMERGENCIES

In a real fire

Do not wait for this system. Leave the area, raise the alarm and call emergency services: 10177 (fire and ambulance, South Africa) or 112 from a mobile phone. Follow your site's emergency procedures.
